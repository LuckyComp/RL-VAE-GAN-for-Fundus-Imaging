## Encoder Pre-training
As the GAN system relies on the latent space generated by the Encoder, we must either first train the encoder and then proceed to use the weights again during generation of images, or both encoder and SRGAN are trained sequential. In this project we choose to employ the first method rather than the second as it reduces the chance of failures during training, and reduces training times.
The VAE architecture is set up, with 4 Conv2d layers that compress the dimension of the image, followed by a Average Adaptive Pool that further standardizes the latent space dimension so as to minimize issues caused by variation in input dimensions. The decoder follows a similar structure with a upscale layer followed by 4 ConvTranspose2d layers. In the scope of this project the decoder is auxilary to us as we do not wish to reconstruct the image using the decoder.
### Loss Calculation
As is common in VAE we use ELBO loss (Evidence Lower Bound) which is a combination of two losses, a reconstruction loss that affects the decoder, and a KL-Divergence loss that affects the encoder.
```math
L_{ELBO} = L_{recon} + \beta \cdot L_{KL} 
```
Reconstruction loss is calculated by combining MSE for pixel-to-pixel comparisions and SSIM for semantic errors during reconstruction, 
```math
L_{recon} = MSE_{mean} + (0.5) \cdot (1 - SSIM)
```
KL-Divergence loss is calculated to measure how well the encoder has encoded training samples into the latent space.
```math
L_{KL} = -(0.5)\cdot \Sigma (1 + log(\sigma^2) - \mu^2 - \sigma^2)
```
Where $\sigma^2 =$ variance of latent space distribution, $\mu^2=$ mean of latent space distribution.
Since, during the early epochs of pre-training the encoder will not yet have learnt to encode features into the latent space properly, hence it would negatively impact the training if we were to penalize it for the initial loss. As such we use KL-Annealing, this gradually increases the effect of the $L_{KL}$ on the encoder by a factor of $\beta$ . Where, $\beta \in min \to 1$ . 

### Grouping the data and training
Though we have developed the structure of the VAE we must take one more step before we can proceed to training, the data in the RMFi dataset is highly varied in terms of the lighting and the health of the eyes. If a generative model were to be trained on this data directly it would lead to the generation of orange blobs, that represent an average of the entire dataset. To combat this problem we must label & group the data into different classes, to do so manually would be tideous. However we can use unsupervised algorithms to do the same. In this project we use clustering algorithm to group similar points in the latent space and store them into a json file, where every image has a corresponding cluster number, we have split the data here into 5 clusters.

## Generative Model
When choosing a generative model for such a project, there are a few trade-offs that are made, choosing to use a DDPM (Diffusion Model) would indeed provide images of great quality, but it would require a lot of processing power to run such a model, as diffusion models require multiple iterations to destroy an image with noise and recreate it. Conversely, A Generative Adversarial Network works by running two networks adversarially to generate the desired output, it much more hardware effecient but is prone to stability issues such as mode collapse.
